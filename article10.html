Meta Platforms (META) Deep Dive: 7 Checks, Valuation & Our 12-Month Price Target

META is down 23% from its all-time high of $796, trades at a P/E of 22x — cheaper than the S&P 500 — and just reported its fastest revenue growth since 2021. Ninety-six percent of covering analysts rate it a Buy. So why is it still at $620? We run 7 checks to find out whether this pullback is a gift or a warning.

The Company in One Paragraph

Meta Platforms operates the world's largest family of social media applications: Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, and Threads. It generates virtually all of its revenue — $56.3 billion in Q1 2026 — from digital advertising. Meta's 3.56 billion daily active people (DAP) is the largest addressable audience in the history of advertising. The company is in the midst of its largest-ever investment cycle, pouring $125-145 billion into AI infrastructure in 2026 alone, with the thesis that AI will make its advertising dramatically more efficient and eventually power new products like AI assistants and smart glasses.

"Meta is the most unloved AI hyperscaler — and its stock looks like a true bargain."
— The Motley Fool, May 15, 2026

Check #1 — Revenue Growth

Q1 2026: Revenue +33% YoY to $56.31B — Fastest Growth Since 2021
Meta's Q1 2026 revenue of $56.31 billion grew 33% year-over-year, beating analyst estimates of $55.45 billion. On a constant currency basis, growth was 29%. Ad impressions across the Family of Apps increased 19% year-over-year, while average price per ad increased 12%. Together, these drove the revenue acceleration. Q2 2026 guidance of $58-61 billion implies continued 26-32% growth. Full-year 2025 revenue was $200.97 billion (+22% YoY).
✅ PASS — Fastest growth since 2021, with Q2 guidance also strong

Check #2 — User Base & Engagement

3.56 Billion Daily Active People — +4% YoY
Meta's Family daily active people (DAP) reached 3.56 billion in March 2026, up 4% year-over-year. This is an almost incomprehensible number — roughly 44% of humanity uses a Meta product every single day. User growth slowed slightly quarter-over-quarter due to internet disruptions in Iran and WhatsApp restrictions in Russia. The slight sequential dip is noise; the trend is still growth. Threads, Meta's Twitter competitor, continues to grow and is not yet significantly monetized — representing future upside.
✅ PASS — 3.56B DAP is an unassailable competitive moat

Check #3 — Profitability & Operating Margin

Operating Margin 41% — Operating Income +30% to $22.87B
Meta's operating margin held at 41% in Q1 2026, unchanged from the prior year, despite dramatically increased AI infrastructure spending. Operating income grew 30% to $22.87 billion. Net income was $26.77 billion, boosted by an $8.03 billion discrete tax benefit. Excluding the tax benefit, diluted EPS would have been $7.31 — still above the $6.72 estimate. A 41% operating margin on $56 billion in quarterly revenue is exceptional. Few companies this size generate cash at this rate.
✅ PASS — 41% margin maintained despite AI spending surge

Check #4 — Valuation: The Key Argument for META

P/E 22x — 18% Below Historical Average — Cheaper Than S&P 500
This is the most compelling argument for META. At $620, the stock trades at a trailing P/E of approximately 22x on TTM EPS of $27.95. This is 18% below Meta's own 5-year historical average P/E of 23.4x. The S&P 500 trades at approximately 30x earnings. Meta — growing revenue at 33% with a 41% operating margin and 3.56 billion daily users — trades at a discount to the broader market. This is the definition of undervaluation relative to quality.
✅ PASS — Trading at a discount to history and to the market

Check #5 — The CapEx Problem: Why META Is Down 23%

$125-145B CapEx in 2026 — Increased From Prior Guidance
This is the reason META fell from $796 to $613. In Q1 2026, Meta raised its full-year 2026 capital expenditure guidance to $125-145 billion, up from the prior range of $115-135 billion. Investors who had hoped AI spending would peak were disappointed. The concern is legitimate: massive CapEx compresses near-term free cash flow and increases execution risk. However, the bull case is clear — Meta is building infrastructure that will power AI advertising tools, AI assistants, and augmented reality products over the next decade. The question is whether investors trust management to deploy this capital effectively. CEO Zuckerberg's track record — the Reels pivot, the AI ad efficiency gains — suggests they should.
⚠️ WARN — Legitimate near-term concern, but likely to be the entry point in retrospect

Check #6 — AI Integration & Competitive Moat

Advantage+ AI Driving +12% Ad Price Growth — Llama AI Open Source
Meta's AI investments are already generating measurable returns. The Advantage+ advertising suite uses AI to automatically optimize ad campaigns, reducing the cost per result for advertisers. This AI-driven efficiency improvement is the primary driver of the 12% year-over-year increase in average price per ad. More advertisers spending more per ad, powered by AI that actually works. Meta is also open-sourcing its Llama AI models, which drives adoption across developers and positions Meta as an AI ecosystem player. Reality Labs (Quest headsets, Ray-Ban Meta glasses) continues to lose money but the hardware ecosystem builds long-term platform loyalty.
✅ PASS — AI is already monetized, not just promised

Check #7 — Analyst Sentiment & Institutional Positioning

96% Buy Rate — 78 Analysts — Median Target $856 (+38% Upside)
Among 78 Wall Street analysts covering META, 61 rate it a Buy, 6 rate it a Hold, and 0 rate it a Sell. The 96% buy rate is among the highest of any S&P 500 stock. The median price target is $856, implying 38% upside from $620. The highest target is $1,144. Even after the CapEx-driven target reductions, the consensus remains firmly bullish. Major funds including Viking Global and Glenview Capital recently increased their Meta stakes. Berkshire's new CEO is among those reshuffling positions. Institutional conviction remains high.
✅ PASS — Near-unanimous Wall Street conviction at current prices

STRONG BUY
12-Month Price Target: $800 | Current Price: ~$620 | Upside: ~29%

META at $620 is one of the most straightforward value propositions in large-cap technology. The stock has fallen 23% from its high for a single reason: CapEx anxiety. The business itself — 33% revenue growth, 41% operating margins, 3.56 billion daily users, AI already monetized — is arguably the strongest it has ever been.

The 22x P/E is historically cheap for Meta and lower than the S&P 500 average. The 96% analyst buy rate and $856 consensus target reflect near-universal institutional conviction. Our conservative 12-month target of $800 requires only a re-rating to Meta's historical average P/E — no heroic growth assumptions required.

The main risk is that CapEx continues to rise and compresses earnings through 2026-2027, delaying the re-rating. Even in that scenario, the long-term thesis — AI-powered advertising + 3.56B users — remains intact.

For investors with a 12-24 month horizon, current levels represent an attractive entry point. The optimal strategy is to accumulate at $580-620 and hold through the CapEx cycle. By 2027-2028, when AI infrastructure spending begins to generate measurable returns, the market is likely to re-rate META significantly higher.
